1//! # The transition rotation — from real, through RoPE, to `SO(4)`
2//!
3//! This module owns the data-dependent rotation of the Mamba-3 transition:
4//! the four [`RotationKind`]s, their cache accumulator ([`RotationState`]),
5//! their per-step definition ([`RotationSpec`]) and the one entry point that
6//! applies them to `B`/`C` ([`rotate_bc_forward`]).
7//!
8//! The data-dependent RoPE of Mamba-3 gives a **complex-valued** SSM. The
9//! state transition factors as a per-head scalar decay times a block-diagonal
10//! of `2×2` rotations (paper Prop. *Complex-to-Real SSM Equivalence*).
11//! `SO(2) ≅ U(1)` is **abelian**, so the cumulative rotation collapses to a
12//! sum of angles, and `B`/`C` absorb it (the "RoPE trick", Prop. *Complex SSM,
13//! Data-Dependent RoPE Equivalence*). See
14//! [`crate::mamba3::rotation::rope::apply_rope`].
15//!
16//! The next rung of the ladder is a **quaternion** (`k = 4`) rotational state:
17//! the rotation of the transition is in the left-isoclinic subgroup
18//! `SU(2) ⊂ SO(4)` instead of `SO(2)`. Unit quaternions under multiplication
19//! are `SU(2)`, which is **non-abelian** and has non-solvable finite subgroups
20//! (the binary icosahedral group `2I = SL(2,5)`, a double cover of `A₅`). By
21//! Barrington's theorem, this lifts the state-tracking that the layer can
22//! reach from the solvable/`TC⁰` regime (parity, mod-k) toward `NC¹`, which
23//! abelian rotations provably cannot reach.
24//!
25//! ## What survives, what changes
26//!
27//! The key fact follows from telescoping and orthogonality only, **without**
28//! commutativity: the RoPE *factoring* survives intact. With the **ordered**
29//! cumulative rotation `Pₜ = Rₜ Rₜ₋₁ ⋯ R₁`,
30//!
31//! ```text
32//!   Cₜᵀ (Rₜ⋯Rᵢ₊₁) Bᵢ  =  (Pₜᵀ Cₜ)ᵀ (Pᵢᵀ Bᵢ)  =  C̄ₜᵀ B̄ᵢ ,
33//! ```
34//!
35//! so the scalar-decay SSD core (`L ⊙ C̄B̄ᵀ`) is **unchanged**. Only the
36//! projections `B̄ᵢ = Pᵢᵀ Bᵢ`, `C̄ₜ = Pₜᵀ Cₜ` turn. What is lost is the
37//! closed-form sum of angles. The cumulative rotation comes from an
38//! **associative scan over the per-step quaternions** ([`quat_cumprod`]). A
39//! product of unit quaternions is again a unit quaternion, so the scan needs no
40//! `wrap_angle`. Its rounding drift is small, and [`rotate_bc_forward`]
41//! renormalises the prefixes. The cross-chunk carry is one quaternion per
42//! block/head, the analogue of [`RotationState::Angle`].
43//!
44//! `SO(2)` ([`apply_rope`](rope::apply_rope)) is the abelian collapse. If each
45//! quaternion has one fixed axis, they commute, and [`quat_cumprod`] reduces to
46//! a sum of half-angles (asserted in the tests).
47//!
48//! ## Pipeline (the `k = 4` instantiation of the rotation block)
49//!
50//! ```text
51//!   per-step unit quaternion qₜ      (materialise from the in-projection; caller)
52//!        │  quat_cumprod (assoc. scan, + cross-chunk carry)
53//!        ▼
54//!   cumulative rotation Qₜ
55//!        │  rotate_state_rank_blocks(B, conj(Qₜ)) , rotate_state_rank_blocks(C, conj(Qₜ))
56//!        ▼
57//!   B̄, C̄  ──►  standard scalar-decay SSD  (unchanged)
58//! ```
59//!
60//! For [`RotationKind::Rotor4D`], the same pipeline runs with the two factors
61//! stacked along the block axis. The last step becomes the two-sided
62//! `rotate_state_rank_blocks_two_sided(B, conj(Qₜ), Tₜ)`.
63//!
64//! ## `SO(4)`: the whole rotation group of a block ([`RotationKind::Rotor4D`])
65//!
66//! `SU(2)` is only half of the rotations of a 4-block. The general element of
67//! `SO(4) ≅ (SU(2)×SU(2))/±1` is the **two-sided** product
68//!
69//! ```text
70//!   Rₜ(v) = qₜ ⊗ v ⊗ p̄ₜ            (a rotor; left factor q, right factor p)
71//! ```
72//!
73//! and everything above survives it, because the factoring uses only "the
74//! per-step maps compose, and each is orthogonal". Composing,
75//!
76//! ```text
77//!   Pₜ(v) = Qₜ ⊗ v ⊗ T̄ₜ ,   Qₜ = qₜ⊗⋯⊗q₁ ,  Tₜ = pₜ⊗⋯⊗p₁
78//!   Pₜ⁻¹(v) = Qₜ* ⊗ v ⊗ Tₜ    ⇒   B̄ᵢ = Qᵢ* ⊗ Bᵢ ⊗ Tᵢ ,  C̄ₜ likewise
79//! ```
80//!
81//! The conjugation reverses the right-hand order **twice**, so `T` accumulates
82//! by the *same* left fold as `Q`: one [`quat_cumprod`] over a doubled block
83//! axis, not a second, reversed scan. The cost over
84//! [`Quaternion4D`](RotationKind::Quaternion4D) is twice the generator
85//! channels, twice the block axis of the scan, and one extra [`quat_mul`] per
86//! `B`/`C` application. The SSD core is still unchanged.
87//!
88//! Why do this, when the left and right factors *commute* with each other and
89//! so add no "more non-abelianness"? Left multiplication is **isoclinic**:
90//! `L_q` turns both invariant planes of the block by the same angle. So `SU(2)`
91//! cannot give two independent plane angles, and it does not contain the
92//! abelian `SO(2)²` rotation that it generalises. The right factor opens the
93//! maximal torus (plane angles `a−b`, `a+b` for the half-angles of `q`, `p`),
94//! so the ladder `Complex2D ⊂ Rotor4D ⊃ Quaternion4D` is real. It also contains
95//! the adjoint action `v ↦ q ⊗ v ⊗ q̄`, a faithful `SO(3)` on the imaginary part
96//! of the block. So a group like `A₅` can be tracked as itself, not through its
97//! double cover `2I`, where `±g` are one element but two different states.
98//! Also, as a representation of `SU(2)×SU(2)`, `ℍ` is the irreducible tensor
99//! product `(½,½)`, not `(½,0) ⊕ (0,½)`: no arrangement of left-only blocks
100//! reproduces it.
101//!
102//! `SO(4)` is the ceiling for `k = 4` (the largest norm-preserving transition
103//! group of a block). `k = 4` is the last rung with a cheap closed form: at
104//! `k = 8` the octonions are non-associative, and the scan itself breaks.
105//!
106//! ## The bottom rung: [`RotationKind::Real1D`]
107//!
108//! `k = 1`, the trivial group: no rotation, a purely real transition. It is the
109//! ablation that the ladder is measured against. It is *structural*, not a
110//! zeroed knob: the in-projection has no rotation channels, the cache holds no
111//! accumulator ([`RotationState::Real`]), and `B`/`C` reach the SSD core
112//! unchanged. So to switch the rotation off, select the *kind*.
113//! `rope_fraction` only narrows a rotation that exists. `Real1D` is also the
114//! one kind that makes no pairs, so it is the only one that accepts an **odd**
115//! `state_rank`, down to the scalar state `state_rank = 1`.
116//!
117//! Quaternion layout: the last axis has size 4 and holds `(w, x, y, z)`, with
118//! `w` the real part. A `state_rank` of `r = 4·J` is `J` independent quaternion
119//! blocks. The rotation acts within each block, exactly as RoPE acts within
120//! each `2`-pair. [`Mamba3`](crate::mamba3::mamba3::Mamba3) selects a kind with
121//! [`RotationKind`] and applies it through one [`RotationSpec`]. The SSD
122//! kernels do not depend on the kind.

138/// Which rotational-state algebra the block uses for the data-dependent
139/// transition rotation that `B`/`C` absorb.
140///
141/// - [`Real1D`](RotationKind::Real1D) — the trivial group: no rotation. The
142///   transition is the plain scalar decay, a **real** SSM (the transition of
143///   Mamba-2 under the trapezoid of Mamba-3). The in-projection has *no*
144///   rotation channels, and the cache holds no accumulator
145///   ([`RotationState::Real`]). This is the rotation ablation, which is why
146///   `rope_fraction` has no `0.0` setting.
147/// - [`Complex2D`](RotationKind::Complex2D) — the abelian `SO(2)`/complex RoPE
148///   of Mamba-3: cumulative *angles* (a prefix sum), applied by
149///   [`apply_rope`](crate::mamba3::rotation::rope::apply_rope). The default.
150/// - [`Quaternion4D`](RotationKind::Quaternion4D) — the non-abelian
151///   `SU(2) ⊂ SO(4)` quaternion rotation: a cumulative *product* by
152///   [`quat_cumprod`], applied by [`rotate_state_rank_blocks`]. Richer
153///   state-tracking. It uses the [`RotationState::Quaternion`] accumulator.
154/// - [`Rotor4D`](RotationKind::Rotor4D) — the **whole** rotation group of a
155///   4-block, `SO(4) ≅ (SU(2)×SU(2))/±1`: a *two-sided* quaternion product
156///   `v ↦ q ⊗ v ⊗ p̄`. Two per-step quaternions instead of one (twice the
157///   generator channels, one scan over a doubled block axis). It uses the
158///   [`RotationState::Rotor`] accumulator.
159///
160/// The kinds are a ladder, `Real1D ⊂ Complex2D ⊂ Rotor4D` and
161/// `Real1D ⊂ Quaternion4D ⊂ Rotor4D`, but the middle two are
162/// **incomparable**, which is why `Rotor4D` exists. Left multiplication is
163/// *isoclinic*: `L_q` turns both invariant planes of the block by the same
164/// angle (`L_i` sends `1↦i` **and** `j↦k`). So `Quaternion4D` cannot express
165/// two independent per-pair angles: it does not contain the abelian rotation
166/// that it generalises. The right factor opens the full maximal torus (plane
167/// angles `a−b` and `a+b` for half-angles `a`, `b`), and with it every element
168/// of `SO(4)`.
169#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq, Eq, Default, serde::Serialize, serde::Deserialize)]
170pub enum RotationKind {
171    /// No rotation: a real transition (the trivial group). No rotation
172    /// channels, no cumulative accumulator, `B`/`C` go through unchanged.
173    Real1D,
174    /// Abelian complex (`SO(2)`) RoPE. The default.
175    #[default]
176    Complex2D,
177    /// Non-abelian quaternion (`SU(2)`, left-isoclinic) rotation.
178    Quaternion4D,
179    /// The full `SO(4)` rotation of each 4-block: two-sided `v ↦ q ⊗ v ⊗ p̄`.
180    Rotor4D,
181}
182
183impl RotationKind {
184    /// How many cumulative-rotation quaternions the accumulator holds per
185    /// (head, 4-block): one for [`Quaternion4D`](RotationKind::Quaternion4D),
186    /// **two** for [`Rotor4D`](RotationKind::Rotor4D) (the left and right
187    /// factors). Meaningless for the non-quaternion kinds
188    /// ([`Complex2D`](RotationKind::Complex2D) accumulates angles,
189    /// [`Real1D`](RotationKind::Real1D) accumulates nothing), where it is 1.
190    ///
191    /// The factors are stacked along one block axis. So every quaternion
192    /// primitive (the generator split, [`quat_from_scaled_axis`], the
193    /// [`quat_cumprod`] scan, [`quat_normalize`]) runs **once** over
194    /// `quat_factors · blocks` blocks and needs no `Rotor4D` branch. Only the
195    /// *application* to `B`/`C` is different.
196    pub fn quat_factors(self) -> usize {
197        match self {
198            RotationKind::Rotor4D => 2,
199            RotationKind::Real1D | RotationKind::Complex2D | RotationKind::Quaternion4D => 1,
200        }
201    }
202}

204/// Everything the rotation needs from the block: which algebra, how much of
205/// `state_rank` it turns, and how far one step can turn it.
206///
207/// [`Mamba3::rotation_spec`](crate::mamba3::mamba3::Mamba3::rotation_spec)
208/// makes it, and [`rotate_bc_forward`], the one site that computes the
209/// per-step rotation, takes it. `step` calls it too (its token is a sequence of
210/// `micro_steps` positions), so `forward` and `step` cannot drift apart here.
211#[derive(Debug, Clone, Copy, PartialEq)]
212pub struct RotationSpec {
213    /// Which rotational-state algebra ([`RotationKind`]).
214    pub kind: RotationKind,
215    /// How many leading `state_rank` entries turn (see
216    /// [`Mamba3Config::rope_fraction`](crate::mamba3::mamba3::Mamba3Config::rope_fraction)).
217    /// For the quaternion kinds this is a multiple of 4. For
218    /// [`RotationKind::Real1D`] it is `0`, and nothing reads it.
219    pub rope_dim: usize,
220    /// The per-step rotation bound in half-turns per unit `Δ`
221    /// ([`Mamba3Config::rotation_range`](crate::mamba3::mamba3::Mamba3Config::rotation_range)):
222    /// one step turns by at most `range · π · Δ`.
223    pub range: f64,
224}

228/// converts between backends by `Clone`.
229#[derive(Module, Debug, Default)]

232/// The cumulative-rotation accumulator that a Mamba-3 cache carries between
233/// calls: the variant that matches the [`RotationKind`] of the block.
234///
235/// - [`Real`](RotationState::Real) — nothing: a real transition has no
236///   cumulative rotation to carry.
237/// - [`Angle`](RotationState::Angle) — abelian per-pair cumulative RoPE angle,
238///   shape `[batch, nheads, num_rope_angles]`.
239/// - [`Quaternion`](RotationState::Quaternion) — per-block cumulative unit
240///   quaternion, shape `[batch, nheads, blocks, 4]`, from [`quat_cumprod`].
241/// - [`Rotor`](RotationState::Rotor) — both `SO(4)` factors, shape
242///   `[batch, nheads, 2·blocks, 4]`.
243///
244/// This is the cache-level counterpart of [`RotationKind`].
245/// [`RotationState::identity`] builds the fresh accumulator of any kind.
246#[derive(Module, Debug)]
247pub enum RotationState {
248    /// The empty accumulator of [`RotationKind::Real1D`]: a real transition
249    /// composes nothing between calls. It holds a [`NoRotation`] only because
250    /// the Burn `Module` derive takes exactly one field per enum variant.
251    Real(NoRotation),
252    /// Abelian RoPE cumulative angle, shape `[batch, nheads, num_rope_angles]`.
253    Angle(Tensor<3>),
254    /// Quaternion cumulative rotation, shape `[batch, nheads, blocks, 4]`.
255    Quaternion(Tensor<4>),
256    /// `SO(4)` cumulative rotation, shape `[batch, nheads, 2·blocks, 4]`: the
257    /// left factors `Qₜ = qₜ⊗⋯⊗q₁` in the first `blocks` entries of the block
258    /// axis, the right factors `Tₜ = pₜ⊗⋯⊗p₁` in the second (see
259    /// [`split_rotor`]).
260    ///
261    /// One tensor, not two, so the scan, the normalisation and the cache
262    /// plumbing stay single-call. The conjugation in `v ↦ q v p̄` reverses the
263    /// right-hand order **twice**, so `T` accumulates with the same left-fold
264    /// as `Q`, and no reversed scan is necessary.
265    Rotor(Tensor<4>),
266}
267
268impl RotationState {
269    /// The empty accumulator of [`RotationKind::Real1D`].
270    pub fn real() -> Self {
271        RotationState::Real(NoRotation)
272    }
273
274    /// Zero-initialised abelian angle accumulator `[batch, nheads, num_rope_angles]`.
275    pub fn zeros_angle(
276        batch: usize,
277        nheads: usize,
278        num_rope_angles: usize,
279        device: &Device,
280    ) -> Self {
281        RotationState::Angle(Tensor::zeros([batch, nheads, num_rope_angles], device))
282    }
283
284    /// Identity-initialised quaternion accumulator `[batch, nheads, blocks, 4]`
285    /// (every block is the identity quaternion `(1, 0, 0, 0)`).
286    pub fn identity_quaternion(
287        batch: usize,
288        nheads: usize,
289        blocks: usize,
290        device: &Device,
291    ) -> Self {
292        let w = Tensor::ones([batch, nheads, blocks, 1], device);
293        let xyz = Tensor::zeros([batch, nheads, blocks, 3], device);
294        RotationState::Quaternion(Tensor::cat(vec![w, xyz], 3))
295    }

297    /// The variant's name, for assertion messages.
298    fn variant(&self) -> &'static str {
299        match self {
300            RotationState::Real(_) => "Real",
301            RotationState::Angle(_) => "Angle",
302            RotationState::Quaternion(_) => "Quaternion",
303            RotationState::Rotor(_) => "Rotor",
304        }
305    }
306
307    /// Check that this is the empty [`Real`](RotationState::Real) accumulator,
308    /// and return it. Panics on any other variant, so a cache built for a
309    /// rotating kind cannot go into a [`Real1D`](RotationKind::Real1D) block.
310    pub fn expect_real(self) -> Self {
311        match self {
312            RotationState::Real(_) => self,
313            other => panic!("RotationState is {}, expected Real", other.variant()),
314        }
315    }
316
317    /// Unwrap the abelian angle accumulator. Panics on any other variant.
318    pub fn angle(self) -> Tensor<3> {
319        match self {
320            RotationState::Angle(a) => a,
321            other => panic!("RotationState is {}, expected Angle", other.variant()),
322        }
323    }
324
325    /// Identity-initialised `SO(4)` accumulator `[batch, nheads, 2·blocks, 4]`
326    /// (both factors of every block are the identity quaternion `(1, 0, 0, 0)`).
327    pub fn identity_rotor(batch: usize, nheads: usize, blocks: usize, device: &Device) -> Self {
328        let w = Tensor::ones([batch, nheads, 2 * blocks, 1], device);
329        let xyz = Tensor::zeros([batch, nheads, 2 * blocks, 3], device);
330        RotationState::Rotor(Tensor::cat(vec![w, xyz], 3))
331    }
332
333    /// The identity accumulator for `kind` — the one place a fresh cache's
334    /// rotation state is built, for every pathway and both cache types.
335    pub fn identity(
336        kind: RotationKind,
337        batch: usize,
338        nheads: usize,
339        num_rope_angles: usize,
340        num_quat_blocks: usize,
341        device: &Device,
342    ) -> Self {
343        match kind {
344            RotationKind::Real1D => RotationState::real(),
345            RotationKind::Complex2D => {
346                RotationState::zeros_angle(batch, nheads, num_rope_angles, device)
347            }
348            RotationKind::Quaternion4D => {
349                RotationState::identity_quaternion(batch, nheads, num_quat_blocks, device)
350            }
351            RotationKind::Rotor4D => {
352                RotationState::identity_rotor(batch, nheads, num_quat_blocks, device)
353            }
354        }
355    }
356
357    /// Unwrap the quaternion accumulator. Panics on any other variant.
358    pub fn quaternion(self) -> Tensor<4> {
359        match self {
360            RotationState::Quaternion(q) => q,
361            other => panic!("RotationState is {}, expected Quaternion", other.variant()),
362        }
363    }
364
365    /// Unwrap the `SO(4)` accumulator (`[batch, nheads, 2·blocks, 4]`, both
366    /// factors stacked). Panics on any other variant.
367    pub fn rotor(self) -> Tensor<4> {
368        match self {
369            RotationState::Rotor(q) => q,
370            other => panic!("RotationState is {}, expected Rotor", other.variant()),
371        }
372    }
373
374    /// The stacked quaternion accumulator for `kind`, and the number of
375    /// **state** 4-blocks it covers: half its block axis for
376    /// [`RotationKind::Rotor4D`], which stacks two factors there.
377    ///
378    /// Panics on a variant that the kind does not use. The two variants have
379    /// the same rank and differ only in the length of one axis. Without the
380    /// check, a mismatched cache would be silently reinterpreted.
381    fn quat_stack(self, kind: RotationKind) -> (Tensor<4>, usize) {
382        match (kind, self) {
383            (RotationKind::Quaternion4D, RotationState::Quaternion(q)) => {
384                let blocks = q.dims()[2];
385                (q, blocks)
386            }
387            (RotationKind::Rotor4D, RotationState::Rotor(q)) => {
388                let stacked = q.dims()[2];
389                assert_eq!(stacked % 2, 0, "a Rotor accumulator stacks two factors");
390                (q, stacked / 2)
391            }
392            (kind, other) => panic!(
393                "RotationState is {}, which is not {kind:?}'s accumulator",
394                other.variant()
395            ),
396        }
397    }
398
399    /// Run the [`NaN`/`Inf` guards](burn_stack::modules::misc::sanity) on the held tensor.
400    pub fn sanity(&self) {
401        match self {
402            RotationState::Real(_) => {}
403            RotationState::Angle(a) => burn_stack::modules::sanity(a),
404            RotationState::Quaternion(q) | RotationState::Rotor(q) => burn_stack::modules::sanity(q),
405        }
406    }
407}

413/// Hamilton product `a ⊗ b` of two quaternion tensors.
414///
415/// Both inputs have shape `[..., 4]`, with the last axis ordered
416/// `(w, x, y, z)`. The product is component-wise and broadcasts over the
417/// leading dims. Quaternion multiplication is **non-commutative**
418/// (`a ⊗ b ≠ b ⊗ a` in general) but associative.
419///
420/// With `ℝ⁴` identified with the quaternions, left-multiplication `v ↦ a ⊗ v`
421/// is exactly the action of the `4×4` rotation matrix [`quat_to_rot4`]`(a)`.
422/// So this is also how a rotation is *applied* to a state/`B`/`C` block (see
423/// [`rotate_state_rank_blocks`]).
424pub fn quat_mul<const D: usize>(a: Tensor<D>, b: Tensor<D>) -> Tensor<D> {
425    let n = D - 1;
426    let aw = a.clone().narrow(n, 0, 1);
427    let ax = a.clone().narrow(n, 1, 1);
428    let ay = a.clone().narrow(n, 2, 1);
429    let az = a.narrow(n, 3, 1);
430    let bw = b.clone().narrow(n, 0, 1);
431    let bx = b.clone().narrow(n, 1, 1);
432    let by = b.clone().narrow(n, 2, 1);
433    let bz = b.narrow(n, 3, 1);
434
435    // Hamilton product (each term is shape [..., 1]).
436    let w = aw.clone() * bw.clone()
437        - ax.clone() * bx.clone()
438        - ay.clone() * by.clone()
439        - az.clone() * bz.clone();
440    let x = aw.clone() * bx.clone() + ax.clone() * bw.clone() + ay.clone() * bz.clone()
441        - az.clone() * by.clone();
442    let y = aw.clone() * by.clone() - ax.clone() * bz.clone()
443        + ay.clone() * bw.clone()
444        + az.clone() * bx.clone();
445    let z = aw * bz + ax * by - ay * bx + az * bw;
446
447    Tensor::cat(vec![w, x, y, z], n)
448}

450/// Quaternion conjugate `q* = (w, −x, −y, −z)` (shape `[..., 4]`).
451///
452/// For a **unit** quaternion, `q* = q⁻¹`, and the rotation matrix satisfies
453/// `Lₚ⋆ = Lₚᵀ = Lₚ⁻¹`. So the rotation by the *inverse* cumulative rotation
454/// (`B̄ = Pᵀ B`) is `rotate_state_rank_blocks(B, conj(Q))`.
455pub fn quat_conj<const D: usize>(q: Tensor<D>) -> Tensor<D> {
456    let n = D - 1;
457    let w = q.clone().narrow(n, 0, 1);
458    let xyz = q.narrow(n, 1, 3);
459    Tensor::cat(vec![w, -xyz], n)
460}

462/// Normalise quaternions to unit norm along the last axis (shape `[..., 4]`).
463///
464/// The block normalises each materialised quaternion here, so it is a real
465/// unit quaternion (an element of `SU(2)`). A tiny floor guards the zero
466/// quaternion.
467pub fn quat_normalize<const D: usize>(q: Tensor<D>) -> Tensor<D> {
468    let n = D - 1;
469    // Clamp the sum-of-squares *before* `sqrt`. At a zero quaternion the
470    // forward `sqrt(0)=0` is fine, but the `sqrt` backward is `1/(2·0)=∞`, and
471    // `∞·(2·0)=NaN`. A pre-`sqrt` clamp puts the degenerate point in the flat
472    // region of `clamp_min`, so its gradient is a finite 0 (and a unit
473    // quaternion, sumsq=1, is unchanged). The floor also keeps `norm` away
474    // from 0 for the division.
475    //
476    // The floor is the dtype-aware `div_eps` on the *sum-of-squares* (a norm
477    // floor of `√div_eps`). It must be a representable normal in the working
478    // dtype. In f16 a `div_eps²`-sized floor (~5e-7) would underflow below the
479    // min-normal (~6.1e-5) and silently do nothing. So the floor on the
480    // squared quantity is `div_eps` itself, which is above the denormal floor
481    // of each format by construction.
482    let eps = burn_stack::utils::div_eps(q.dtype());
483    let norm = (q.clone() * q.clone()).sum_dim(n).clamp_min(eps).sqrt();
484    q / norm
485}
486
487/// Euclidean norm over the last axis, computed **scale-free** so it cannot
488/// overflow. The components are divided by their (detached) largest magnitude
489/// before the squaring, and the result is scaled back.
490///
491/// A square of the raw components is the obvious way, and the wrong one here,
492/// because the inputs are raw in-projection channels. `‖r‖²` overflows f32 at
493/// `|r| ≈ 2e19` and **f16 at `|r| ≈ 250`**, an ordinary activation. The
494/// overflow is silent: `∞` divides back to `0`, so a very large generator
495/// would give *no* rotation, the opposite of the intended "turn as far as the
496/// bound allows". The fp16 path of [`RmsNorm`](burn_stack::modules::RmsNorm)
497/// uses the same trick for the same reason.
498///
499/// A `div_eps` floor applies to the sum of squares before the `sqrt`. So a
500/// zero vector is in the flat region of `clamp_min` and backpropagates a
501/// finite `0`, not the singular `1/(2·0)` of `sqrt`.
502///
503/// # Shapes
504/// - `t`  : `[..., n]`
505/// - out  : `[..., 1]`
506pub(crate) fn safe_norm<const D: usize>(t: Tensor<D>) -> Tensor<D> {
507    let n = D - 1;
508    let eps = burn_stack::utils::div_eps(t.dtype());
509    // Detached: the rescaling is a numerical device, not part of the function
510    // being differentiated (`d‖t‖/dt = t̂` either way).
511    let scale = t.clone().abs().max_dim(n).detach() + eps; // [..., 1]
512    let unit = t / scale.clone(); // components ≤ 1
513    (unit.clone() * unit).sum_dim(n).clamp_min(eps).sqrt() * scale
514}

516/// Materialise a unit quaternion from a **scaled rotation vector** `g ∈ ℝ³`
517/// (axis · angle) with the exponential map. This is the data-dependent
518/// "materialise `Rₜ`" step, the analogue of the RoPE angle `Δₜ · π · tanh(θₜ)`.
519///
520/// With `‖g‖ = angle` and the axis `ĝ = g / angle`, it returns the unit
521/// quaternion `q = (cos(angle/2), sin(angle/2)·ĝ)`. A vanishing `g` maps to the
522/// identity `(1, 0, 0, 0)`. So when a small `Δₜ` (the discretisation step)
523/// scales `g`, the rotation is near the identity: a small step barely turns
524/// the state. The `sin(angle/2)/angle` factor is the numerically stable form
525/// of the per-component scale near `g = 0` (otherwise `0/0`).
526///
527/// # Shapes
528/// - `g` : `[..., 3]`
529/// - out : `[..., 4]` (ordered `(w, x, y, z)`), unit norm.
530pub fn quat_from_scaled_axis<const D: usize>(g: Tensor<D>) -> Tensor<D> {
531    let n = D - 1;
532    // `safe_norm` guards the origin (a zero generator would otherwise hit the
533    // singular `sqrt` backward and give NaN). It also keeps the sum of squares
534    // from an overflow at large `g`.
535    let angle = safe_norm(g.clone()); // [..., 1]
536    let half = angle.clone() * 0.5;
537    let w = half.clone().cos(); // [..., 1]
538    // sin(angle/2) / angle  → 1/2 as angle → 0 (no rotation). `angle ≥ √div_eps`
539    // after the pre-`sqrt` clamp, so the division is already guarded.
540    let scale = half.sin() / angle; // [..., 1]
541    let v = g * scale; // [..., 3]
542    quat_normalize(Tensor::cat(vec![w, v], n))
543}

545/// Bound the **magnitude** of a rotation vector and keep its direction:
546/// returns `max_angle · tanh(‖r‖) · r̂`.
547///
548/// This is the quaternion counterpart of the abelian `π·tanh(ϑ)`, with a
549/// deliberate difference. A squash of each of the three raw channels
550/// *separately* would bound the rotation vector to a **cube**. The reachable
551/// angle would then depend on the axis (`max_angle` about a coordinate axis,
552/// but `√3·max_angle` about the diagonal). Worse, a per-component `tanh` also
553/// moves the *direction*, so the axis that a projection selects would depend
554/// on the size of the projection. A bound on the norm keeps the axis exactly
555/// `r̂` and makes the angle a function of `‖r‖` only, so axis and angle are
556/// independent knobs.
557///
558/// Near `r = 0` the map is `≈ max_angle · r` (the `tanh(n)/n → 1` limit). The
559/// sum-of-squares floor is the same pre-`sqrt` clamp as in
560/// [`quat_from_scaled_axis`]. It puts the degenerate point in the flat region
561/// of `clamp_min`, with the correct finite gradient.
562///
563/// # Shapes
564/// - `r`  : `[..., 3]`
565/// - out  : `[..., 3]`, with `‖out‖ ≤ max_angle` (attained once `tanh` saturates).
566pub fn bound_rotation_vector<const D: usize>(r: Tensor<D>, max_angle: f64) -> Tensor<D> {
567    let norm = safe_norm(r.clone()); // [..., 1]
568    let scale = norm.clone().tanh() * max_angle / norm;
569    r * scale
570}

572/// Materialise the `4×4` orthogonal matrix of left-multiplication by `q`.
573///
574/// Maps `q` of shape `[..., 4]` to `[..., 4, 4]` such that, for `v` of shape
575/// `[..., 4]`, `Lq · v == quat_mul(q, v)`.  Concretely (rows = output coords,
576/// cols = input coords, all in `(w, x, y, z)` order):
577///
578/// ```text
579///   ⎡ w  -x  -y  -z ⎤
580///   ⎢ x   w  -z   y ⎥
581///   ⎢ y   z   w  -x ⎥
582///   ⎣ z  -y   x   w ⎦
583/// ```
584///
585/// For a unit `q` this is orthogonal with `det = 1` (a left-isoclinic
586/// rotation). It exists mainly for verification. The cheap way to apply a
587/// rotation is [`rotate_state_rank_blocks`] (a quaternion product, no `4×4`
588/// matrix). `DR` must equal `D + 1`.
589pub fn quat_to_rot4<const D: usize, const DR: usize>(q: Tensor<D>) -> Tensor<DR> {
590    assert_eq!(D + 1, DR, "quat_to_rot4 maps rank D to rank D+1");
591    let n = D - 1;
592    let w = q.clone().narrow(n, 0, 1);
593    let x = q.clone().narrow(n, 1, 1);
594    let y = q.clone().narrow(n, 2, 1);
595    let z = q.narrow(n, 3, 1);
596
597    // Each row is a [..., 4] tensor (the four column entries).
598    let row0 = Tensor::cat(vec![w.clone(), -x.clone(), -y.clone(), -z.clone()], n);
599    let row1 = Tensor::cat(vec![x.clone(), w.clone(), -z.clone(), y.clone()], n);
600    let row2 = Tensor::cat(vec![y.clone(), z.clone(), w.clone(), -x.clone()], n);
601    let row3 = Tensor::cat(vec![z, -y, x, w], n);
602
603    // Stack the rows along a freshly inserted row axis → [..., 4, 4].
604    Tensor::cat(
605        vec![
606            row0.unsqueeze_dim::<DR>(n),
607            row1.unsqueeze_dim::<DR>(n),
608            row2.unsqueeze_dim::<DR>(n),
609            row3.unsqueeze_dim::<DR>(n),
610        ],
611        n,
612    )
613}

619/// Apply a per-block quaternion rotation to the `state_rank` axis of `v`.
620///
621/// `v` has shape `[..., state_rank]` with `state_rank = 4·J`, seen as `J`
622/// independent quaternion blocks. `q` has shape `[..., J, 4]` (one unit
623/// quaternion per block, the same leading dims as `v`). Returns `q ⊗ v` per
624/// block (the rotation `L_q` within each `4`-block), reshaped back to
625/// `[..., state_rank]`.
626///
627/// This generalises the per-pair `2×2` rotation of RoPE to per-block `4×4`.
628/// To absorb the *inverse* cumulative rotation into `B`/`C` (`B̄ = Pᵀ B`), pass
629/// `q = conj(Qcum)`: `rotate_state_rank_blocks(b, conj(qcum))`.
630///
631/// `DB` must equal `D + 1` (the block-split inserts the `J` axis).
632pub fn rotate_state_rank_blocks<const D: usize, const DB: usize>(
633    v: Tensor<D>,
634    q: Tensor<DB>,
635) -> Tensor<D> {
636    assert_eq!(
637        D + 1,
638        DB,
639        "rotate_state_rank_blocks splits one axis into (J, 4)"
640    );
641    let dims = v.dims();
642    let state_rank = dims[D - 1];
643    assert_eq!(
644        state_rank % 4,
645        0,
646        "state_rank must be a multiple of 4 (quaternion blocks)"
647    );
648    let blocks = state_rank / 4;
649
650    // Build the block-split shape [..., J, 4] (rank DB) and the flat shape
651    // [..., state_rank] (rank D) for the round trip.
652    let mut split_shape = [0usize; DB];
653    split_shape[..D - 1].copy_from_slice(&dims[..D - 1]);
654    split_shape[DB - 2] = blocks;
655    split_shape[DB - 1] = 4;
656
657    let v_blocks = v.reshape(split_shape); // [..., J, 4]
658    let rotated = quat_mul(q, v_blocks); // L_q applied per block
659    rotated.reshape(dims) // [..., state_rank]
660}

662/// Apply a per-block **two-sided** quaternion rotation to the `state_rank` axis
663/// of `v`: `v ↦ ql ⊗ v ⊗ qr` per 4-block.
664///
665/// This is the general `SO(4)` element ([`RotationKind::Rotor4D`]). Pass
666/// `qr = (1,0,0,0)` to get [`rotate_state_rank_blocks`]. To absorb the
667/// *inverse* cumulative rotation into `B`/`C` (`B̄ = P⁻¹B` with
668/// `P(v) = Q v T̄`), pass `ql = conj(Q)`, `qr = T`. The conjugate is on the
669/// **left** factor only.
670///
671/// Shapes as [`rotate_state_rank_blocks`]: `v` is `[..., state_rank]`, both
672/// quaternions `[..., J, 4]`, and `DB = D + 1`.
673pub fn rotate_state_rank_blocks_two_sided<const D: usize, const DB: usize>(
674    v: Tensor<D>,
675    ql: Tensor<DB>,
676    qr: Tensor<DB>,
677) -> Tensor<D> {
678    assert_eq!(
679        D + 1,
680        DB,
681        "rotate_state_rank_blocks_two_sided splits one axis into (J, 4)"
682    );
683    let dims = v.dims();
684    let state_rank = dims[D - 1];
685    assert_eq!(
686        state_rank % 4,
687        0,
688        "state_rank must be a multiple of 4 (quaternion blocks)"
689    );
690
691    let mut split_shape = [0usize; DB];
692    split_shape[..D - 1].copy_from_slice(&dims[..D - 1]);
693    split_shape[DB - 2] = state_rank / 4;
694    split_shape[DB - 1] = 4;
695
696    let v_blocks = v.reshape(split_shape); // [..., J, 4]
697    quat_mul(quat_mul(ql, v_blocks), qr).reshape(dims)
698}

700/// Split a stacked [`RotationState::Rotor`] accumulator `[..., 2·J, 4]` into
701/// its `(left, right)` factors, each `[..., J, 4]`.
702pub fn split_rotor<const D: usize>(q: Tensor<D>) -> (Tensor<D>, Tensor<D>) {
703    let stacked = q.dims()[D - 2];
704    assert_eq!(stacked % 2, 0, "a Rotor accumulator stacks two factors");
705    let blocks = stacked / 2;
706    (
707        q.clone().narrow(D - 2, 0, blocks),
708        q.narrow(D - 2, blocks, blocks),
709    )
710}

712// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
713// Cumulative rotation scan (the associative, non-abelian replacement for cumsum)
714// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
715
716/// Cumulative (ordered, left-accumulating) quaternion product along the
717/// sequence axis, with a cross-chunk carry.
718///
719/// This is the non-abelian analogue of the cumulative *sum of angles* of
720/// RoPE. Complex rotations compose by adding angles. Quaternions compose by
721/// multiplication, which depends on the order, so a real scan is necessary.
722///
723/// # Shapes
724/// - `q_bshj4` : `[batch, sequence, nheads, J, 4]` per-step **unit** quaternions
725///   (block count `J = state_rank / 4`).
726/// - `init`    : optional carry `[batch, nheads, J, 4]` — the cumulative
727///   rotation at the end of the previous chunk (identity `(1,0,0,0)` for a fresh
728///   start).
729/// - returns `(cum, final_carry)` where `cum` is `[batch, sequence, nheads, J, 4]`
730///   with `cum[:, t] = qₜ ⊗ qₜ₋₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ q₀ ⊗ init` (newest on the left, matching
731///   `Pₜ = Rₜ ⋯ R₁`), and `final_carry` `[batch, nheads, J, 4]` is `cum[:, −1]`
732///   to thread into the next chunk.
733///
734/// A run over a split sequence, with `final_carry` passed between the parts,
735/// is exactly equal to a run over the whole sequence (asserted in the tests).
736/// This is the chunked-prefill / streaming guarantee for the rotation
737/// accumulator.
738///
739/// The implementation is a **Hillis–Steele** inclusive associative scan. The
740/// quaternion product is associative (but not commutative), so a log-depth
741/// scan applies if the operand order stays newest-on-left. Each doubling step
742/// is one full-tensor [`quat_mul`] plus a sequence shift. So the *sequential
743/// dependency depth* is `O(log sequence)`, not the `O(sequence)` of a
744/// token-by-token loop: the same values, from a few large batched kernels
745/// instead of thousands of small serialized ones (and a shallow autodiff
746/// graph). A sequential reference (`quat_cumprod_sequential` in the tests
747/// module) is the test oracle, asserted equal on values **and** gradients.
748pub fn quat_cumprod(q_bshj4: Tensor<5>, init: Option<Tensor<4>>) -> (Tensor<5>, Tensor<4>) {
749    let [batch, sequence, nheads, blocks, _four] = q_bshj4.dims();
750    let device = q_bshj4.device();
751
752    // Pure prefix product Pₜ = qₜ ⊗ qₜ₋₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ q₀ by Hillis–Steele doubling.
753    // Invariant after each step with offset `d`: a[t] holds the product of the
754    // window [t .. max(t-2d+1, 0)] (newest on the left). After ⌈log₂ sequence⌉
755    // doublings the window covers [t .. 0], i.e. a[t] = Pₜ.
756    let mut a = q_bshj4;
757    let mut offset = 1usize;
758    while offset < sequence {
759        // shifted[t] = a[t-offset] for t ≥ offset, else the identity quaternion
760        // (1,0,0,0) — so the first `offset` prefixes pass through unchanged
761        // (a ⊗ identity = a).
762        let ident = {
763            let w = Tensor::ones([batch, offset, nheads, blocks, 1], &device);
764            let xyz = Tensor::zeros([batch, offset, nheads, blocks, 3], &device);
765            Tensor::cat(vec![w, xyz], 4)
766        };
767        let shifted = Tensor::cat(vec![ident, a.clone()], 1).narrow(1, 0, sequence);
768        // Recent block (a) on the left, older block (shifted) on the right.
769        a = quat_mul(a, shifted);
770        offset *= 2;
771    }
772
773    // Fold the cross-chunk carry once: cumₜ = Pₜ ⊗ init. `init` (the previous
774    // chunk's final cumulative rotation) is the oldest factor, hence on the
775    // right; a missing carry is the identity and needs no multiply.
776    let cum = match init {
777        Some(init_bhj4) => {
778            assert_eq!([batch, nheads, blocks, 4], init_bhj4.dims());
779            quat_mul(a, init_bhj4.unsqueeze_dim::<5>(1)) // [batch, 1, nheads, J, 4] broadcasts over seq
780        }
781        None => a,
782    };
783
784    let final_carry = cum.clone().narrow(1, sequence - 1, 1).squeeze_dim::<4>(1); // [batch, nheads, J, 4]
785    (cum, final_carry)
786}

792/// Apply a per-block quaternion rotation to the first `rope_width` entries of
793/// the `state_rank` axis (a multiple of 4). The rest is unchanged. The
794/// quaternion analogue of [`apply_rope_partial`].
795///
796/// `q` has one quaternion per rotated block (`rope_width / 4` of them). `DB`
797/// must equal `D + 1`.
798pub fn rotate_blocks_partial<const D: usize, const DB: usize>(
799    v: Tensor<D>,
800    q: Tensor<DB>,
801    rope_width: usize,
802) -> Tensor<D> {
803    let r = v.dims()[D - 1];
804    debug_assert!(
805        rope_width > 0,
806        "a quaternion kind always turns at least one block; a real transition is RotationKind::Real1D"
807    );
808    if rope_width == r {
809        rotate_state_rank_blocks::<D, DB>(v, q)
810    } else {
811        let head = v.clone().narrow(D - 1, 0, rope_width);
812        let tail = v.narrow(D - 1, rope_width, r - rope_width);
813        let head_rot = rotate_state_rank_blocks::<D, DB>(head, q);
814        Tensor::cat(vec![head_rot, tail], D - 1)
815    }
816}

818/// Two-sided counterpart of [`rotate_blocks_partial`]: rotates the first
819/// `rope_width` entries of the `state_rank` axis by `v ↦ ql ⊗ v ⊗ qr`. The
820/// rest is unchanged.
821pub fn rotate_blocks_two_sided_partial<const D: usize, const DB: usize>(
822    v: Tensor<D>,
823    ql: Tensor<DB>,
824    qr: Tensor<DB>,
825    rope_width: usize,
826) -> Tensor<D> {
827    let r = v.dims()[D - 1];
828    debug_assert!(rope_width > 0, "see rotate_blocks_partial");
829    if rope_width == r {
830        rotate_state_rank_blocks_two_sided::<D, DB>(v, ql, qr)
831    } else {
832        let head = v.clone().narrow(D - 1, 0, rope_width);
833        let tail = v.narrow(D - 1, rope_width, r - rope_width);
834        let head_rot = rotate_state_rank_blocks_two_sided::<D, DB>(head, ql, qr);
835        Tensor::cat(vec![head_rot, tail], D - 1)
836    }
837}

841// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

843/// The abelian per-step angle increment `θ̂ₜ = Δₜ · range·π·tanh(ϑₜ)`, one per
844/// head and rotation pair.
845///
846/// `DP1 = D + 1`: the head axis is inserted before the pair axis, so a
847/// sequence-shaped call (`rot [b, s, a]`, `dt [b, s, h]`) yields `[b, s, h, a]`
848/// and a single-token call (`rot [b, a]`, `dt [b, h]`) yields `[b, h, a]`.
849pub fn angle_increment<const D: usize, const DP1: usize>(
850    rot: Tensor<D>,
851    dt: Tensor<D>,
852    range: f64,
853) -> Tensor<DP1> {
854    assert_eq!(D + 1, DP1, "angle_increment inserts the head axis");
855    let bounded = rot.tanh() * (range * std::f64::consts::PI);
856    dt.unsqueeze_dim::<DP1>(D) * bounded.unsqueeze_dim::<DP1>(D - 1)
857}
858
859/// The quaternion per-step rotation vector `gₜ = Δₜ · range·π·tanh(‖r‖)·r̂`, one
860/// per head and quaternion block (feed it to [`quat_from_scaled_axis`]).
861///
862/// The bound is on the magnitude, not on each component, so the axis is
863/// exactly the direction of the projection (see [`bound_rotation_vector`]).
864///
865/// Unlike the abelian [`angle_increment`], the generators are projected **per
866/// head**: `rot` has `nheads · 3 · J` channels. So every head turns about its
867/// own data-dependent axis, instead of one shared axis with only a different
868/// `Δ`. A shared axis would make the rotations of the heads a one-parameter
869/// family. The abelian path can accept that: its rotations commute, so a
870/// per-head angle is the only freedom. For a non-abelian transition, the axis
871/// *is* the expressive part. Two heads that turn about different axes track
872/// different words, which is why there is more than one head.
873///
874/// `DP1 = D + 1`, `DP2 = D + 2`: a sequence-shaped call (`rot [b, s, h·3·J]`,
875/// `dt [b, s, h]`) yields `[b, s, h, J, 3]` and a single-token call
876/// (`rot [b, h·3·J]`, `dt [b, h]`) yields `[b, h, J, 3]`.
877pub fn generator_increment<const D: usize, const DP1: usize, const DP2: usize>(
878    rot: Tensor<D>,
879    dt: Tensor<D>,
880    blocks: usize,
881    range: f64,
882) -> Tensor<DP2> {
883    assert_eq!(D + 1, DP1, "generator_increment splits (3·J) into (J, 3)");
884    assert_eq!(
885        D + 2,
886        DP2,
887        "generator_increment also splits off the head axis"
888    );
889    let dims = rot.dims();
890    let nheads = dt.dims()[D - 1];
891    assert_eq!(
892        dims[D - 1],
893        nheads * 3 * blocks,
894        "the rotation channels are three generators per (head, quaternion block)"
895    );
896    // [..., h·3·J] → [..., h, J, 3]
897    let mut split = [0usize; DP2];
898    split[..D - 1].copy_from_slice(&dims[..D - 1]);
899    split[DP2 - 3] = nheads;
900    split[DP2 - 2] = blocks;
901    split[DP2 - 1] = 3;
902    let bounded = bound_rotation_vector::<DP2>(rot.reshape(split), range * std::f64::consts::PI);
903    // Δ is per head: broadcast it over (J, 3)
904    bounded * dt.unsqueeze_dim::<DP1>(D).unsqueeze_dim::<DP2>(D + 1)
905}

907// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
908// Forward / step rotation of B and C (shared by both SSD pathways)
909// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
910
911/// Rotate `B`/`C` for a **full sequence** by the data-dependent transition
912/// rotation. Returns the rotated projections and the new cumulative
913/// [`RotationState`] for the cache. `step` calls it too, with the `u`
914/// positions of its token.
915///
916/// Branches on [`RotationKind`]:
917/// - [`Real1D`](RotationKind::Real1D): nothing happens. `B`/`C` go through,
918///   and the (empty) accumulator comes back. `rot` is `None` there, because
919///   the block projects no rotation channels.
920/// - [`Complex2D`](RotationKind::Complex2D): the abelian RoPE of the
921///   reference. The cumulative angle (`helpers::prefix_sum`, continued from `prev`),
922///   then [`apply_rope_partial`].
923/// - [`Quaternion4D`](RotationKind::Quaternion4D): the per-step unit quaternion
924///   [`quat_from_scaled_axis`] (the in-projection generators, scaled per head
925///   by `Δ`), composed by [`quat_cumprod`] from the cached quaternion. Then
926///   `rotate(·, conj(Qₜ))` on the first `4·blocks` state-rank entries of
927///   `B`/`C`.
928/// - [`Rotor4D`](RotationKind::Rotor4D): the same scan over both stacked
929///   factors, then the two-sided `Q* ⊗ · ⊗ T`.
930///
931/// `B` turns at every position of the folded sequence, because it writes at
932/// all of them. `C` turns only where it is **read**: one position per
933/// `read_stride` (`micro_steps`, where `1` keeps the two axes equal). The
934/// cumulative rotation covers the whole folded axis either way. `C` takes a
935/// stride slice of it. See [the read axis](crate::mamba3::product).
936///
937/// The `mimo_rank` axis is **broadcast**, never indexed: no rotation count has
938/// it, in any branch. This is necessary, not a convenience. The `M` ranks
939/// share one state, so they share its transition, and per-rank angles have no
940/// state-space preimage (`info/mamba-3/mimo-as-batch.md` §7).
941///
942/// # Shapes
943/// - `rot_bsa` : `[batch, sequence, num_rotation_channels]`: the in-projection
944///   rotation channels (angles for Complex2D, quaternion generators for the
945///   quaternion kinds). `None` for Real1D, which projects none.
946/// - `dt_bsh`  : `[batch, sequence, nheads]` (`Δ`).
947/// - `b_bsmhr` : `[batch, sequence, mimo_rank, nheads, state_rank]`.
948/// - `c_btmhr` : `[batch, sequence / read_stride, mimo_rank, nheads, state_rank]`.
949pub fn rotate_bc_forward(
950    rot_bsa: Option<Tensor<3>>,
951    dt_bsh: Tensor<3>,
952    prev: RotationState,
953    b_bsmhr: Tensor<5>,
954    c_btmhr: Tensor<5>,
955    read_stride: usize,
956    spec: RotationSpec,
957) -> (Tensor<5>, Tensor<5>, RotationState) {
958    let [batch, sequence, mimo_rank, nheads, _state_rank] = b_bsmhr.dims();
959    let tokens = c_btmhr.dims()[1];
960    let RotationSpec {
961        kind,
962        rope_dim,
963        range,
964    } = spec;
965    // Only `Real1D` projects no rotation channels, and it never reads them.
966    let rot = |r: Option<Tensor<3>>| r.expect("a rotating kind projects rotation channels");
967    match kind {
968        RotationKind::Real1D => (b_bsmhr, c_btmhr, prev.expect_real()),
969        RotationKind::Complex2D => {
970            let rot_bsa = rot(rot_bsa);
971            let prev_angle_bha = prev.angle();
972            let num_rope_angles = prev_angle_bha.dims()[2];
973            let raw_angles_bsha = angle_increment::<3, 4>(rot_bsa, dt_bsh, range);
974            // The abelian scan is blocked (the quaternion scan below is
975            // log-depth). It is *not* `Tensor::cumsum`, whose cost is
976            // quadratic in the sequence length here. The angle of the cache
977            // is the carry-in of the scan, so it joins the block offset and
978            // needs no add of its own. See [`prefix_sum`].
979            let cum_angles_bsha = prefix_sum::<4, 5>(
980                raw_angles_bsha,
981                1,
982                Some(prev_angle_bha.unsqueeze_dim::<4>(1)),
983            );
984            let cum_angles_bsmha = cum_angles_bsha.clone().unsqueeze_dim::<5>(2).expand([
985                batch,
986                sequence,
987                mimo_rank,
988                nheads,
989                num_rope_angles,
990            ]);
991            let rotate_pairwise = mimo_rank == 1;
992            let b = apply_rope_partial::<5>(
993                b_bsmhr,
994                cum_angles_bsmha.clone(),
995                rope_dim,
996                rotate_pairwise,
997            );
998            // `C` reads at one folded position per token, so it turns by the
999            // cumulative angle of that position only. That is `u`× less work
1000            // than a turn of a broadcast copy at every micro-step.
1001            let cum_angles_btmha =
1002                helpers::read_rows::<5, 6>(cum_angles_bsmha, 1, read_stride);
1003            let c = apply_rope_partial::<5>(c_btmhr, cum_angles_btmha, rope_dim, rotate_pairwise);
1004            let last = wrap_angle(
1005                cum_angles_bsha
1006                    .narrow(1, sequence - 1, 1)
1007                    .squeeze_dim::<3>(1),
1008            );
1009            (b, c, RotationState::Angle(last))
1010        }
1011        RotationKind::Quaternion4D | RotationKind::Rotor4D => {
1012            // `stack` is the generator/scan block axis: `blocks` for
1013            // Quaternion4D, `2·blocks` for Rotor4D (left factors, then right).
1014            // Everything up to the *application* is factor-agnostic and runs
1015            // once over the whole stack.
1016            let (prev_q_bhk4, blocks) = prev.quat_stack(kind);
1017            let stack = prev_q_bhk4.dims()[2];
1018            // The rotated width is a whole number of quaternion blocks, and
1019            // the cache was sized for exactly that many.
1020            assert_eq!(rope_dim, blocks * 4, "cache/block rotation width mismatch");
1021            let rope_width = rope_dim;
1022            // Generators [b,s,h,stack,3]: the raw channels, bounded to an
1023            // angle of at most `range·π` (see `generator_increment`) and scaled
1024            // per head by Δ. The bound is necessary: an unbounded `g = rot·Δ`
1025            // overflows f32 to `inf` for a large in-projection activation, and
1026            // the `cos(∞)` in `quat_from_scaled_axis` then gives a forward NaN.
1027            let g_bshk3 = generator_increment::<3, 4, 5>(rot(rot_bsa), dt_bsh, stack, range);
1028            let q_step_bshk4 = quat_from_scaled_axis::<5>(g_bshk3);
1029            // Memory-efficient scan: a custom recompute backward (it saves only
1030            // the leaf inputs), not the intermediates of the scan. Equal to
1031            // [`quat_cumprod`] on values and gradients (asserted in tests).
1032            let (cum_bshk4, final_bhk4) = crate::mamba3::quat_scan::quat_cumprod_recalculated(
1033                q_step_bshk4,
1034                Some(prev_q_bhk4),
1035            );
1036            // Renormalise the prefixes. In exact arithmetic, a product of unit
1037            // quaternions is a unit quaternion. But the scan composes each
1038            // prefix from `⌈log₂ L⌉` multiplies, so the norm drifts: very
1039            // little in f32, ~1% over a long f16 sequence. A non-unit rotation
1040            // rescales B/C instead of only turning them. `forward` and `step`
1041            // both come here, so they stay numerically alike and orthogonal.
1042            let cum_bshk4 = quat_normalize(cum_bshk4);
1043            let over_mimo = |q_bshj4: Tensor<5>| {
1044                q_bshj4
1045                    .unsqueeze_dim::<6>(2)
1046                    .expand([batch, sequence, mimo_rank, nheads, blocks, 4])
1047            };
1048            // `C`'s copy, at the one folded position per token it reads at.
1049            let over_mimo_read = |q_bshj4: Tensor<5>| {
1050                helpers::read_rows::<5, 6>(q_bshj4, 1, read_stride)
1051                    .unsqueeze_dim::<6>(2)
1052                    .expand([batch, tokens, mimo_rank, nheads, blocks, 4])
1053            };
1054            // B̄ = P⁻¹B: rotate by the inverse cumulative rotation, per block,
1055            // broadcast over the mimo_rank axis.
1056            let (b, c) = match kind {
1057                RotationKind::Rotor4D => {
1058                    // P(v) = Q v T̄  ⇒  P⁻¹(v) = Q* v T (conjugate on the left
1059                    // factor only).
1060                    let (left, right) = split_rotor(cum_bshk4);
1061                    let left = quat_conj(left);
1062                    let (ql_read, qr_read) =
1063                        (over_mimo_read(left.clone()), over_mimo_read(right.clone()));
1064                    let (ql, qr) = (over_mimo(left), over_mimo(right));
1065                    (
1066                        rotate_blocks_two_sided_partial::<5, 6>(b_bsmhr, ql, qr, rope_width),
1067                        rotate_blocks_two_sided_partial::<5, 6>(
1068                            c_btmhr, ql_read, qr_read, rope_width,
1069                        ),
1070                    )
1071                }
1072                _ => {
1073                    let conj_bshk4 = quat_conj(cum_bshk4);
1074                    let conj_btmhj4 = over_mimo_read(conj_bshk4.clone());
1075                    let conj_bsmhj4 = over_mimo(conj_bshk4);
1076                    (
1077                        rotate_blocks_partial::<5, 6>(b_bsmhr, conj_bsmhj4, rope_width),
1078                        rotate_blocks_partial::<5, 6>(c_btmhr, conj_btmhj4, rope_width),
1079                    )
1080                }
1081            };
1082            let final_bhk4 = quat_normalize(final_bhk4);
1083            let state = match kind {
1084                RotationKind::Rotor4D => RotationState::Rotor(final_bhk4),
1085                _ => RotationState::Quaternion(final_bhk4),
1086            };
1087            (b, c, state)
1088        }
1089    }
1090}
