# 04: One K1–K3 backward for both pathways

Status: resolved

Blocked by: `10-one-standard-ssd-scan.md`

From the architecture review of 2026-11-03. Line numbers are those of commit
`42ca3d0e`. Not compiled.

**Files:** `src/mamba3/double_ssd/ssd/serial_recalculated/combined_backward.rs:256-306,412-543`,
`src/mamba3/single_ssd/ssd/serial_recalculated/combined_backward.rs:418-539`.

**Problem:** 566 lines hide a difference of a few terms: `y_diag`, `scale`, the strict
mask and the seeds. Normalised, 194 of 271 double-SSD lines occur in single-SSD. The
forward K1–K4 and `k4_ssd_state_passing_backward` are already shared. The K1 suffix
sum, the K2 backward and BLUE are copies. K3 differs only in `k_scaled` and the
`d_scale` split. A K3 fix needs two edits (`e43b32e3`, `0ac8e539`). Single-SSD lacks
three `san` guards that double-SSD has (`d_c_blue`, `diff`, `d_da_blue`).

**Direction:** The K1, K2, K3 and BLUE backwards move beside their forwards in
`double_ssd/ssd/serial_recalculated/serial_recalculated.rs`. Each
`combined_backward` becomes a short assembly.

**ADR-0007 holds:** each fast form still meets autodiff on its own simple form.

**Cost:** Helpers must take tensors by value and drop them at the same points. A
clone that a caller holds raises the GPU peak. The user checks it with `./bench.sh`.

**A later step, worth exploring:** Mamba-2 and the Mamba-3 copy of the standard SSD scan
share one implementation. By reading, with no numeric check, the Mamba-3 kernels compute
the Mamba-2 K1–K5 at `m = 1` and `u = 1`, with `v = dt·x` and `da = dt·A`, plus the D
skip. It needs a Mamba-2 bench by the user, and the fast form stops mirroring
`_mamba_chunk_scan_combined_bwd` line by line.

### Agent brief

**Category:** enhancement

**Summary:** One K1, K2, K3 and BLUE backward serves both Mamba-3 pathways. K5 joins
them if the conditions below hold. This brief replaces the **Direction** and the
**Cost** above. Ticket 10 replaces the **later step**.

**Current behaviour:**

- The two Mamba-3 `combined_backward` functions hold copies of the K1, K2, K3 and BLUE
  backwards.
- The two K5 chunk-group passes also hold copies, around a term that differs: ORANGE on
  double-SSD and LOWER on single-SSD.

**Desired behaviour:**

- Each of the K1, K2, K3 and BLUE backwards exists once, beside its forward, in the
  module that ticket 10 creates. `k3_ssd_chunk_state_extended` is beside
  `k3_ssd_chunk_state`.
- Each `combined_backward` assembles those functions and adds only the terms of its
  pathway.
- The K5 pass follows the decision below.
- The outputs, the final caches and the gradients of every family and SSD path stay the
  same.

**Key interfaces:** The names follow `k4_ssd_state_passing_backward`, and the agent can
change them.

| Function | Takes | Returns |
|---|---|---|
| `k1_ssd_chunk_cumsum_backward` | `d_da_cumsum_bhnl` | `d_da_bnlh` |
| `k2_ssd_bmm_backward` | `d_cb_bnhTMLM`, `c_bntmhr`, `b_bnlmhr` | `d_c_bntmhr`, `d_b_bnlmhr` |
| `k3_ssd_chunk_state_backward` | `d_intra_chunk_state_bnhpr`, the K3 intermediates, `v_bnlmhp`, the K3 input projection | `d_v_bnlmhp`, the gradient of that projection, `d_da_cumsum_bhnl` |
| BLUE, for one chunk group | `d_y_bnhTMp`, `da_cumsum_bnhTM`, `c_bnhTMr`, `chunk_input_state_bnhpr`, `read_stride` | `d_chunk_input_state_bnhpr`, `d_c_bnhTMr`, `d_da_bnhl` |

Double-SSD gives `b_bnlmhr` to K3. Single-SSD gives `k_scaled_bnlmhr`, and it splits the
returned gradient into `d_b` and `d_scale` itself.

**The K5 decision:** Think about this before you write code. The maintainer is not sure
that K5 can move.

By reading, and not compiled:

- These parts are the same in both pathways:
  - the group slices and the `da_cumsum` setup
  - the decay with its mask
  - the split of `d_da` into its target and source terms
- ORANGE has mask diagonal 1. LOWER has mask diagonal 0.
- LOWER has a `scale` per source position and returns `d_scale`. With no scale, its
  statements reduce to those of ORANGE: `prod` is `w`, and `d_prod` is `d_w`.

Share the K5 pass only if all of these conditions hold:

1. Double-SSD holds no extra score-shaped tensor and no extra clone of one.
2. Each pathway keeps its operations and their order.
3. The function takes the scale and the mask diagonal as plain arguments, with no
   callback.
4. The result is easier to read than the two copies.

If a condition fails, keep the ORANGE and LOWER math in each pathway. Share only the
parts that are the same. Write the failed condition in the ticket. Both outcomes meet
this brief.

**Constraints:**

- The shared functions keep the `san` guards of double-SSD. Single-SSD gains them under

- The forward K5 of each pathway stays separate.
